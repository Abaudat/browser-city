# Story 1.3: Scheduled-Reducer Timing Fidelity

A harness, not an anecdote: every number below was emitted by
`scripts/dev/run-sched-timing-spike.sh`, re-runnable with one command. The
raw per-fire observations it exported are committed alongside this file
under `docs/spikes/1.3-scheduled-reducer-timing/*.csv`, and
`republish-summary.json` alongside them carries the republish leg's raw
before/after/fired record.

## Pre-registered budget

Written before this run's numbers were read, per the acceptance
criteria's own instruction not to declare "acceptable" after the fact.

- **Per-fire dispatch budget: 2.5 real seconds.** FR1 fixes the
  compression ratio at 24 in-city minutes per real minute (60 real
  minutes = 1 in-city day = 1440 in-city minutes). A citizen whose
  scheduled arrival is off by up to one in-city minute reads as "on time"
  to a player who is not staring at a clock; one in-city minute is 2.5
  real seconds. Any single fire landing within 2.5s of its scheduled time
  is within what the design can already absorb.
- **Non-compounding requirement: no unit, a shape.** A repeating cadence
  (`ScheduleAt::Interval`, or a day-night/transit system built the same
  way) must not accumulate lateness tick over tick. Even a per-tick
  increment far under the 2.5s budget is unacceptable if it is systematic,
  because a long-running cadence (a session-length L2 tick, the day-night
  cycle) will walk arbitrarily far from its origin given enough ticks
  regardless of how small each individual increment is.
- **If either is violated,** this report says so plainly and the finding
  is recorded against risk R10 and decision D7: the Alarm Clock decision
  is reopened. No compensating correction, catch-up term or smoothing is
  added in this PR to make the number look better (Quentin's and Tim's
  direction, both).

## Method

Three quantities, never conflated in one column (per fired `observation`
row, written by the module's own `fire_probe` reducer -- see
`server/spikes/sched_timing/src/lib.rs`):

- **Dispatch drift**: `ctx.timestamp` (the reducer's own view of "now")
  minus the row's scheduled time, read entirely inside the module. For a
  `ScheduleAt::Time` row this is the row's own field; for a
  `ScheduleAt::Interval` row (which carries a relative duration, not an
  absolute next-fire time) it is measured against an anchor computed
  independently of the platform's own reschedule arithmetic:
  `run_start + tick_number * bucket_ms`, so a compounding reschedule shows
  up as growing drift against this fixed origin rather than being
  absorbed by the anchor itself.
- **Commit lateness** is the same quantity for practical purposes here:
  the module never touches a network or filesystem, so "dispatch" and
  "commit" do not diverge measurably at this workload.
- **Observer lateness**: when a subscribed client actually saw the
  resulting row, via `spacetime subscribe`, each line stamped with the
  wall-clock micros it arrived at. Local loopback, one machine, so client
  and server share a clock -- the one place in this spike a client
  wall-clock read is the right tool, because the acceptance criterion is
  phrased about what a player (on the far side of a subscription) sees.

Two probe shapes cover both scheduling paths the game uses:
`ONESHOT_CHAINED` (`ScheduleAt::Time`, the reducer reinserts a fresh
one-shot `bucket_ms` after its own fire time -- a hand-rolled repeat) and
`INTERVAL` (`ScheduleAt::Interval`, the platform's own repeat). Comparing
the two answers whether compounding, if present, is a property of
`ScheduleAt::Interval` specifically or of scheduled dispatch generally.

Three legs:

1. **Idle ladder** -- both probe shapes, at 100ms/500ms/1s/10s/30s/60s/600s,
   on an otherwise-idle instance.
2. **Under load** -- the fast end of the same ladder (100ms/500ms/1s),
   repeated with a burst of 1,000 and then 10,000 rows independently due
   in the same tick.
3. **Republish while pending** -- one-shots seeded across
   5s/15s/30s/60s/120s/300s/600s plus one repeating row, republished over
   (no `--delete-data`) before any of them fire, then observed. Recorded,
   not asserted: survived, fired late, fired on time, or vanished are four
   different platform behaviours, not one "survived" bucket.

legs' batch and the republish leg's one-shots). For `interval`, `drift`
is cumulative slip against the row's own original anchor (`run_start +
tick * bucket_ms`), not per-tick jitter -- see Method above and the
"cumulative slip" line under each `interval` row, which reports the
per-tick rate directly. Below `n=20` no percentile is shown (Quentin's
direction); the raw observations are printed instead.

batch due in one tick: median 19.6ms at 1,000 rows, median 154ms (max

single fire. The `+9 to +11 ms/tick` compounding rate on `interval` is

if `ONESHOT_CHAINED`-shaped code is ever load-bearing at v1's full
citizen count.

## Republish leg

Seeded 7 `BURST` one-shots (due at 5s/15s/30s/60s/120s/300s/600s) plus
one `INTERVAL` row (1s cadence), then republished the same module over
the same database (no `--delete-data`) immediately, before any of them
fired, then observed for 650s. Recorded, not asserted (raw record:
`docs/spikes/1.3-scheduled-reducer-timing/republish-summary.json`):

| Offset | Fired? | Drift at fire |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5s | yes | 11.8ms |
| 15s | yes | 4.2ms |
| 30s | yes | 4.2ms |
| 60s | yes | 16.2ms |
| 120s | yes | 14.3ms |
| 300s | yes | 6.2ms |
| 600s | yes | 2.5ms |

**All seven survived and fired within 2.5-16ms of their original
schedule.** None vanished, none fired late by anything a player would
notice, none needed a reconciler to rebuild them. This holds across the
whole offset range up to the full 10 minutes -- the longest-pending row
(600s) fired the *most* accurately (2.5ms), not the least.

The repeating row also survived (still pending, at tick 643, when the
window closed) and kept firing -- but its very first post-republish tick
landed 443ms late against its original anchor (versus a handful of
milliseconds for the same bucket on the idle leg), then resumed the same
~9.5ms/tick compounding rate as everywhere else. Read together with "all
seven one-shots survived and fired accurately," this reads as **one
bounded scheduler pause during the republish itself (order ~440ms in
this measurement), not data loss and not a catch-up storm** (no burst of
instant re-fires was observed on either probe shape).

Caveat: this republished an *unchanged* module (same wasm, same schema)
via a local `spacetime publish` hot-swap. A schema-changing publish, or a
Maincloud deploy with a different swap mechanism, might behave
differently -- this leg's finding is about *this* republish path, not a
general guarantee.

## Observer lateness

Measured on `idle-interval-*`/`idle-oneshot-*`'s combined stream via
`spacetime subscribe`, wall-clock receipt time minus `fired_at_micros`,
local loopback (client and server share a clock): n=15,516, median
158ms, p95 10.7s, max 19.1s. The heavy tail is far wider than dispatch
drift itself and most plausibly reflects the subscription stream falling
behind under this leg's own fire rate (thousands of rows/second at
points) rather than a platform dispatch fault -- reported as measured,
not adjusted, and not the quantity the verdict below turns on (Tim's
rule: `ctx.timestamp`-based drift is authoritative; observer lateness is
context for what a player's client would actually see on top of it).

## Verdict

**Per-fire dispatch budget (2.5s): held, at every bucket, under load, and
across the republish leg.** No single fire -- one-shot, interval tick, or
burst member -- exceeded roughly 300ms of drift anywhere in this run.

**Non-compounding requirement: violated.** `ScheduleAt::Interval`
compounds lateness at a stable **+9 to +11 ms per tick**, on an idle
instance and under load alike, independent of bucket size -- so the time
to exceed the 2.5s budget is roughly `250 * bucket_ms`: about 26 seconds
of wall-clock time for a 100ms tick, about 4 minutes for a 1s tick, about
40 minutes even for a coarse 10s tick. Over a session-length run this is
not a rounding error: it would walk a naive interval-driven day-night
cycle or L2 tick arbitrarily far from real time, exactly the failure mode
risk **R10** names. The
platform's own issue tracker (`ScheduleAt::Interval` rescheduling from
actual fire time rather than the previous scheduled time) describes this
mechanism and was reported closed before 2.9.0 shipped; this run's
measurement says the behaviour, or something producing the same signature,
is still present on 2.9.0.

**Per this report's pre-registered rule: decision D7 (the Alarm Clock) is
reopened.** No compensating correction, catch-up term or smoothing is
added in this PR. `docs/architecture.md` records the timeless residue
this run leaves behind: the 2.5s per-fire budget, the ban on assuming a
repeating schedule's phase survives more than a few ticks, and the
republish-survival rule this leg measured.
