## Ideas Generated

### Core Fantasy

**[Fantasy #1]: The Citizen, Not The Chosen One**
_Core Loop_: The player inhabits an ordinary life in a city that ran before them and continues without them. Progress is lateral and personal, not heroic.
_Novelty_: Inverts the protagonist assumption baked into nearly every city-scale game. The city is the main character.

**[Fantasy #2]: The Breathing Machine**
_Core Loop_: The city visibly runs its own life whether or not the player participates.
_Novelty_: Simulation legibility as the core aesthetic. Pleasure comes from noticing the machine work, not steering it.

**[Fantasy #3]: Possibility Vertigo**
_Core Loop_: The player repeatedly discovers that assumed scenery is enterable, joinable, or doable.
_Novelty_: Awe generated by scope of permission rather than scope of power.

**[Discovery #4]: The Affordance Ladder**
_Core Loop_: Any object may be one rung on a chain — buy the bike, ride it, meet riders, learn where they ride, discover a district.
_Novelty_: Content structured as chains of permission rather than quests. Discovery is lateral, social, and often terminates in place.

### The City As Institution

**[Systems #5]: Municipal Memory**
_Core Loop_: The city observes events (accidents, congestion, footfall) and alters itself — signage, transit, zoning, policing.
_Novelty_: World authorship is procedural AND reactive. Infrastructure is a slow feedback loop readable like weather.

**[Systems #10]: The Bureaucratic Chain**
_Core Loop_: City responses are simulated as multi-agent institutional processes — investigation, approval, budget, procurement, logistics, labour — each link performed by a citizen, AI or player.
_Novelty_: "City as protagonist" implemented as visible institutional process. You can watch the city think.

**[Systems #11]: Every Link Is A Job**
_Core Loop_: Each node in a chain is an occupation with a work loop. Player careers are slots in the city's own machinery.
_Novelty_: Job content and world simulation are the same system.

**[Systems #12]: Friction As Narrative**
_Core Loop_: Chains stall, get denied, get expedited. Consequences land on citizens who never saw the paperwork.
_Novelty_: Emergent story from institutional latency. Tragedy by procurement delay.

**[Systems #83]: Response Time Is A Budget Line**
_Core Loop_: How fast the ambulance arrives depends on decisions made in a building you have never entered.
_Novelty_: Makes invisible municipal politics viscerally personal.

### Persistence, Sleep, And The Understudy

**[Persistence #13]: The AI Understudy** — ACCEPTED
_Core Loop_: Logging off hands your character to an AI that keeps living your life. You return to discover what you did.
_Novelty_: Absence becomes content. Your own character is a source of surprise.

**[Persistence #14]: Sleep Is Handover** — SUPERSEDED by #23
_Core Loop_: Sleeping equals the understudy taking over, identical to logging off.
_Novelty_: Rejected — forces players to quit every in-game day, which at a 1–2 hour day cycle is an eviction notice.

**[Sleep #15]: Sleep Is Absence, Absence Is Sleep**
_Core Loop_: Your character sleeps precisely when you are not playing. Sleep debt accrues while on, settles while off.
_Novelty_: Collapses the problem — the mechanic for absence and for sleep become the same thing.

**[Sleep #16]: The Understudy Bleeds Through**
_Core Loop_: Refuse to sleep and the AI takes the wheel in micro-moments. You blink and you are two blocks on, holding a coffee you do not remember buying.
_Novelty_: Sleep wall becomes a gradient, and thematically exact — the game about not mastering your life removes your hands by degrees.

**[Sleep #17]: Dreams Are Where You Level**
_Core Loop_: Sleep is playable. Dreams are a surreal low-stakes space where the day consolidates into growth.
_Novelty_: Dead time becomes signature content; progression stays on one character.

**[Sleep #18]: The Night Self** — ACCEPTED, evolved into #23
_Core Loop_: While you sleep you inhabit something that does not progress.
_Novelty_: Answers the progression-split objection by making the second body progression-free.

**[Sleep #19]: Asymmetric Clock**
_Core Loop_: The city's night runs on tighter compression than its day.
_Novelty_: Non-uniform time as a design instrument. Cheap, but makes night a thin place.

**[Sleep #20]: Staggered Sleep Windows** — TRUE BUT INSUFFICIENT
_Core Loop_: Sleep need is per-character, not synchronised. Shift workers, insomniacs, early risers.
_Novelty_: Guarantees a populated world at every hour. Realistic, but does not solve the player's own sleep gap.

**[Sleep #23]: The Night Shift** — ACCEPTED, the solution
_Core Loop_: While your character sleeps, you take over an NPC's working night — drive the night bus, stock the warehouse — inside a window sized to your sleep duration.
_Novelty_: Dead time becomes labour inside the same city. Sleep costs nothing and the world gets a populated night.

**[Sleep #24]: Reciprocal Understudy**
_Core Loop_: AI covers players; players cover AI. Roles persist; occupants swap.
_Novelty_: One mechanic serves absence, sleep, population density, and the AI/player blend simultaneously.

**[Sleep #25]: Bounded Objectives**
_Core Loop_: Each shift is scoped to fit the window, with a clear done-state at wake-up.
_Novelty_: A time-boxed play unit inside an open-ended sandbox.

**[Sleep #26]: Performance Pay**
_Core Loop_: Doing the shift well earns currency for your main character. Badly earns nothing.
_Novelty_: The anti-chaos mechanism is a payslip, not a rules layer.

**[Sleep #33]: Fatigue-Sized Shifts**
_Core Loop_: How tired you are sets how long you sleep, which sets which NPC shifts you are offered.
_Novelty_: Fatigue becomes a content selector rather than a debuff.

**[Systems #32]: Karma With Teeth** — RESOLVED as #42
_Core Loop_: A moral ledger spanning your character and your night-shift borrowings.
_Novelty_: Needed because consequence for borrowed bodies lands on the NPC, not the player.

**[Systems #42]: The Borrowing Licence** — PROPOSED SOLUTION
_Core Loop_: Night-shift access is a revocable trust held at player level. Wreck borrowed lives, lose access to borrowed lives.
_Novelty_: Accountability follows the player and the punishment is losing content. "You may do as you like with your own life; someone else's is on loan."

**[Systems #43]: Investment As Deterrence**
_Core Loop_: Progression makes your character costly to lose, which makes fines, firing, and jail meaningful.
_Novelty_: Progression's primary job is giving consequence something to bite.

**[Refinement #103]: The Conservative Understudy** — ACCEPTED
_Core Loop_: The AI maintains your life; it does not gamble with it. Goes to work, pays rent, eats, sleeps. Never bets the paycheck or quits the job.
_Novelty_: Absence is safe by design. A persistence system players fear is one they refuse to use.

### Verbs And Game Feel

**[Feel #8]: Granular Embodiment**
_Core Loop_: Actions are performed, not triggered — which hand, which grip, which motion.
_Novelty_: Realism as aesthetic rather than difficulty. Closest relative is Receiver 2's manual handling, applied to ordinary life.

**[Feel #9]: UX-Minimal Diegesis**
_Core Loop_: Gamey affordances exist only where UX genuinely breaks without them.
_Novelty_: Abstraction treated as a cost paid reluctantly, not a default vocabulary.

**[Feel #34]: Immersion Is The Point**
_Core Loop_: Granular control exists to make third-person embodiment as immersive as first-person.
_Novelty_: Realism is instrumental, immersion terminal — which makes granularity negotiable where immersion is cheaper elsewhere.

**[Feel #35]: Procedure Over Prompt**
_Core Loop_: An action is worth simulating when it decomposes into ordered steps with state — arrange, grip, ink, apply, re-ink.
_Novelty_: A concrete admission test for granularity: if the action cannot be performed badly, abstract it.

**[Feel #36]: Props With State**
_Core Loop_: Tools deplete and degrade mid-task. The stamp runs dry, the till runs short of change.
_Novelty_: Micro-maintenance as the texture of work. Competence is remembering the ink.

**[Verbs #27]: The Unnecessary, Done Well**
_Core Loop_: The most satisfying actions are the socially optional ones — the courtesy, the pause, the tidy-up.
_Novelty_: Inverts the optimisation instinct. The good move and the efficient move differ.

**[Verbs #28]: Presence Verbs**
_Core Loop_: Sit. Order. Wait. Watch the water. Actions whose entire payload is being somewhere.
_Novelty_: Idleness as designed content rather than the gap between content.

**[Verbs #29]: Civic Verbs**
_Core Loop_: Bin the bottle. Hold the door. Give up the seat.
_Novelty_: The commons as an interactive surface, at the scale of a single bottle.

**[Verbs #30]: Conversation As Loitering**
_Core Loop_: Talking is done instead of transacting, chosen sentence by sentence, costing time.
_Novelty_: Dialogue priced in the only currency that matters under a compressed clock — minutes.

**[Verbs #31]: Dignity Work**
_Core Loop_: The ramp, the ticket, the correct change. Jobs made of small competent courtesies to specific people.
_Novelty_: Labour as service to individuals rather than resource throughput.

### Work And The Shape Of A Shift

**[Structure #89]: The Shape Of A Shift**
_Core Loop_: Ritual open (arrive, change, equip, handover chat), rhythmic duties (rounds), discretionary middle, ritual close (final round, handover, change out, goodbye).
_Novelty_: A reusable template for every job. Bookends carry meaning; the middle carries freedom.

**[Insight #88]: Boring Jobs Are Free Jobs**
_Core Loop_: Fewer required actions means less supervision means more self-determination. The emptiest shift offers the most choice.
_Novelty_: Inverts the assumption that engagement comes from density of demands.

**[Insight #85]: Boring Jobs Are Observation Posts**
_Core Loop_: Low-action work positions the player as witness — monitors, doorways, routes, windows.
_Novelty_: Turns the genre's most unplayable jobs into the ones best suited to this game's pleasures.

**[Feel #90]: Toys Inside Jobs**
_Core Loop_: Solitaire on the guard post computer. Downtime given something to do rather than designed away.
_Novelty_: Admits boredom exists and makes it inhabitable.

**[Feel #91]: Uniform Ritual**
_Core Loop_: Change in, equip, work, disarm, change out, return equipment.
_Novelty_: Procedure applied to becoming the worker, not just to the work.

**[Design #93]: Self-Imposed Standards**
_Core Loop_: Cleaning the lobby is never required, tracked, or rewarded. It is just cleaner afterwards.
_Novelty_: "The game has no opinion" implemented at the level of a single shift.

**[Design #77]: The Burger Test**
_Core Loop_: Any legitimate job must be intrinsically satisfying enough to compete with the most exciting alternative available.
_Novelty_: A falsifiable standard for whether the procedure-fidelity thesis works.

**[Scope #94]: Job Access Tiers**
_Core Loop_: Some roles stay NPC-only; others open to players. Accessibility is a tuning dial.
_Novelty_: Ship a city with a hundred professions and build ten playable ones.

### Progression, Time, And The Core Loop

**[Loop #44]: Time Is The Currency**
_Core Loop_: Money buys time; time buys discovery. Upgrades measured in minutes returned.
_Novelty_: The compressed clock turns a standard progression treadmill into something genuinely scarce and legible.

**[Loop #45]: The Commute Tax**
_Core Loop_: Where you live and how you travel levy a daily cost in minutes.
_Novelty_: Housing and transport become a single felt optimisation, not a stat sheet.

**[Loop #46]: Rent As Metronome**
_Core Loop_: A recurring obligation that never resolves, giving structure without objectives.
_Novelty_: Pressure without quests. The sandbox has a pulse.

**[Loop #47]: Tier 0**
_Core Loop_: Start materially poor — bad flat, no transport, few clothes, rent due — so the first paycheck lands hard.
_Novelty_: Onboarding through scarcity. The first bike is a bigger moment than most games' endgame.

**[Session #48]: Mum Calls For Dinner**
_Core Loop_: Sessions end because real life ends them, not because the game gates you out.
_Novelty_: Designed for interruptibility, which the understudy makes safe.

**[Endgame #49]: Becoming A Dependency**
_Core Loop_: Late progression means occupying links others need — approving budgets, owning the route, staffing the warehouse. Public office and private ownership are one mechanic under two labels.
_Novelty_: Endgame that fulfils the opening fantasy rather than contradicting it. At hour 100 you are still one part of a massive thing — just a load-bearing one.

**[Endgame #51]: Player Infrastructure**
_Core Loop_: Own the cafe, the building, the depot, the line. Set wages, hours, prices, standards.
_Novelty_: Players become other players' affordance ladders.

### Economy And The Maximalist Problem

**[Economy #105]: The Low Ceiling** — REJECTED
_Core Loop_: A deliberately short wealth ladder that terminates.
_Novelty_: Rejected — the wealth ladder does not terminate. It is simply one goal among several, so money is never the only axis.

**[Economy #106]: Hide The Number**
_Core Loop_: No net worth display, no HUD balance, no leaderboard. Check a statement or count your pocket.
_Novelty_: Follows from UX-Minimal Diegesis. You cannot optimise a number you cannot watch tick.

**[Economy #107]: The Best Things Are Not For Sale**
_Core Loop_: The cycling community, the wooded north, being known at the cafe — none purchasable, only reachable by spending time.
_Novelty_: Makes money structurally the wrong tool for the good content rather than a forbidden one.

**[Economy #108]: Hours Are Capped**
_Core Loop_: Employers demand availability, contracts conflict, fatigue degrades performance, injury costs weeks.
_Novelty_: The 16-hour day fails on its own terms without a moralising system.

**[Design #109]: Steer The Optimiser** — ACCEPTED, chosen lever
_Core Loop_: If something must visibly accrue, let it be districts seen, people who know you, procedures mastered. Optimising those is indistinguishable from playing as intended.
_Novelty_: Redirects the min-maxer instead of fighting them. The grinder becomes a tourist and a regular.

### Failure

**[Failure #71]: Ruin By Process**
_Core Loop_: Failure states are bureaucratic chains run against the player — notice, escalation, judgment, enforcement — each link a moment to intervene.
_Novelty_: No fail-state cliff. You lose slowly, visibly, and with recourse, through the same system that fixes traffic lights.

**[Failure #72]: The Playable Floor**
_Core Loop_: Destitution is an inhabitable state with its own routines, locations, and community.
_Novelty_: The game has no opinion, so poverty is a condition rather than a verdict.

**[Failure #73]: The Net Is Content**
_Core Loop_: Welfare, shelters, and charity are simulated institutions with staffing, budgets, and queues — and player-holdable roles.
_Novelty_: The mechanism rescuing failing players is a career path for thriving ones.

**[Failure #74]: The Night Shift Is The Floor**
_Core Loop_: Borrowed-body work is always available and always pays.
_Novelty_: A no-wall safety valve that already exists for other reasons.

**[Failure #75]: Failure Is Social Glue**
_Core Loop_: Friends lend money, offer a couch, split rent, vouch for you at a job.
_Novelty_: Falling creates the dependency MMOs usually manufacture with raid mechanics.

**[Failure #76]: Your Understudy Struggles Too**
_Core Loop_: Log off while sinking and the AI keeps making decisions with your bad hand.
_Novelty_: Absence has stakes. Bounded by #103 — no catastrophic outcomes.

**[Systems #82]: Injury Costs Time And Money** — ACCEPTED, no death
_Core Loop_: Get hurt, lose days and savings. Hospitals are institutions with paramedic, triage, nurse, surgeon, admin, and billing chains.
_Novelty_: Preserves MMO persistence while keeping realism honest.

### Crime

**[Scope #86]: Lawful Players, v1** — ACCEPTED
_Core Loop_: Players stay on the right side of the law at launch. Crime exists in the world; players engage the response side.
_Novelty_: Ships the crime genre without moderation and population-balance risk.

**[Scope #87]: Decide Crime With Data**
_Core Loop_: Player criminality revisited once real player behaviour is known.
_Novelty_: Treats a major fork as empirical rather than an upfront commitment.

**[Crime #78]: Opportunity, Not Occupation**
_Core Loop_: Crime is available where simulation produces an opening — the unlocked warehouse, the till left open, the corrupt link.
_Novelty_: Population-caps criminality through world state instead of rules or quotas.

**[Crime #79]: Time Is The Punishment**
_Core Loop_: Jail and hospital both cost minutes — the scarcest resource.
_Novelty_: The economy already supplies the harshest penalty; no invented ones needed.

**[Crime #80]: The Record**
_Core Loop_: Convictions persist and close employment doors for a long time.
_Novelty_: Long-tail consequence carried by a physical artefact (a file) rather than a hidden meter.

**[Crime #81]: Crime Happens To The City**
_Core Loop_: Most crime is committed by the simulation; player-facing content is the response — police, detectives, insurance, repair, courts.
_Novelty_: Applies the Accident Investigator model to crime, giving the whole genre without a player criminal class.

### Design Stance

**[Design #37]: The Game Has No Opinion**
_Core Loop_: No decency scoring, no morality meter, no approval. Virtue is the player's business.
_Novelty_: Sandbox stance held rigorously in a genre that almost always installs a judge.

**[Systems #38]: Consequences Need A Physical Carrier**
_Core Loop_: An effect propagates only if something in the world carries it — litter, damage, wear, absence, a filed report. Abstract qualities do not spread.
_Novelty_: One rule making emergence legible and preventing invisible simulation nobody can read.

**[Systems #39]: Broken Windows**
_Core Loop_: A full bin produces one dropped bottle, which licenses the next, and a district degrades — reversibly.
_Novelty_: Neighbourhood character as emergent physical state rather than an authored zone type.

### Multiplayer And Social

**[Multiplayer #6]: Seamless Population**
_Core Loop_: Players and AI citizens occupy the same social space with no marker distinguishing them.
_Novelty_: Removes the NPC/real-person split every MMO makes obvious. Density stops depending on concurrency.

**[Multiplayer #7]: Drop-In Life**
_Core Loop_: Joining and leaving is frictionless — no lobby, no session, no server-select.
_Novelty_: Persistence as ambient rather than a commitment device.

**[Structure #50]: The Understudy Makes Institutions Viable**
_Core Loop_: Player-held links never hard-stall; the AI covers absence. Chains degrade gracefully instead of deadlocking on someone's bedtime.
_Novelty_: The persistence mechanic turns out to be the load-bearing MMO systems solution.

**[Social #52]: Flatshare**
_Core Loop_: Split the rent. Halve the metronome, share the space, argue about the washing up.
_Novelty_: Your first friendship is a financial instrument.

**[Social #53]: Group Leisure**
_Core Loop_: The Sunday cycling outing. Standing arrangements people show up to.
_Novelty_: Social content built on schedules rather than instances — the guild is a routine, not a raid.

**[Social #54]: Two Pairs Of Hands**
_Core Loop_: Work physically requiring coordination — the sofa, the fry station, the beam.
_Novelty_: Co-op emerges from simulation fidelity rather than designed multiplayer content.

**[Social #55]: Hobbies With Verbs**
_Core Loop_: Golf, cycling, racing — shared activities with their own procedures, done for no reason but doing them.
_Novelty_: Leisure treated with the same mechanical seriousness as labour.

**[Social #92]: The Handover**
_Core Loop_: Every shift begins and ends by relieving someone. Optional conversation, information passed, or a nod.
_Novelty_: The AI/player seam is invisible precisely here.

### Architecture And Scope

**[Scope #22]: Simulation To The Floor** — STATED INTENT
_Core Loop_: The chain runs all the way down — the phone gets answered, the truck driven, the pallet moved.
_Novelty_: No abstraction layer between "the city decided" and "a person did it."

**[Scope #56]: Separate The Ledger From The Body** — KEY REFRAME
_Core Loop_: Chains simulate to the floor as records — the investigator filed, the budget cleared, the pallet shipped Tuesday. Bodies instantiate only where observed.
_Novelty_: "Simulated to the bottom" costs a database row, not a rendered human. Truth is cheap; bodies are expensive.

**[Scope #57]: Fidelity Follows Attention**
_Core Loop_: Full procedure near players, statistical everywhere else, reconciled on approach.
_Novelty_: The player is both the camera and the simulation budget.

**[Scope #58]: Players Are The Labour Supply**
_Core Loop_: Night shifts, jobs, and businesses mean players voluntarily perform simulation the engine would otherwise fake.
_Novelty_: The population problem partly solves itself.

**[Architecture #60]: The City Always Ticks**
_Core Loop_: Simulation is continuous and authoritative everywhere; rendering instantiates on demand from computed truth.
_Novelty_: Solves out-of-range events, logged-off persistence, and empty zones with one principle.

**[Architecture #61]: The Truth Test**
_Core Loop_: Would this event have happened identically if no player ever came? Nothing is authored into being by proximity.
_Novelty_: A falsifiable standard for "genuinely simulated" the whole team can check code against.

**[Architecture #62]: Authoritative Sim, Thin Client**
_Core Loop_: The world lives server-side; the browser is a renderer and an input device.
_Novelty_: Serves the 1s boot, the MMO, and anti-cheat simultaneously.

**[Architecture #66]: Resolution Scales, Causality Does Not**
_Core Loop_: Distant simulation is cheaper, never falser. Every state has a cause chain that survives inspection. The disconnected driver resumes where pathfinding, speed, and elapsed time put the car.
_Novelty_: A precise, affordable formulation of "fully simulated."

**[Architecture #67]: One Simulation, Variable Resolution**
_Core Loop_: A single system at different timesteps and detail levels. No separate background approximation.
_Novelty_: Structurally prevents drift between "real" and "faked" regions — you cannot diverge from yourself.

**[Architecture #68]: Kinematic Continuity**
_Core Loop_: Return exactly where cause and elapsed time put you — mid-journey, mid-shift, mid-task.
_Novelty_: Reconnection has no seam because nothing was ever suspended.

**[Systems #21]: Compressed Detached Clock**
_Core Loop_: One in-city day is roughly 1–2 real hours. City time is unmoored from real-world time.
_Novelty_: Everyone rotates through all hours. Night is a place everyone visits.

**[Onboarding #59]: The One-Second Citizen**
_Core Loop_: Tab opens, name entered, you are standing in a city that was already running. Boot in under one second including load.
_Novelty_: The technical constraint enforces the design thesis. No creation ceremony means no protagonist framing.

**[Feel #69]: Density Is Local**
_Core Loop_: Aliveness is measured per screen, not per database. Two tellers, a guard, three in line, a man on a bench.
_Novelty_: Decouples the feeling of massiveness from the cost of massiveness.

**[Feel #70]: The Quiet Periphery**
_Core Loop_: A big city with sparse edges. Empty streets read as character.
_Novelty_: Turns a population budget into intentional geography.

**[Constraint #63]: Agent-Maintainable By Construction**
_Core Loop_: Uniform, data-driven, heavily testable systems. Behaviour as data agents can generate and validate.
_Novelty_: The dev team's nature is itself a design constraint — and record-based simulation is the most agent-friendly choice available.

**[Constraint #64]: Systemic Content Only**
_Core Loop_: No hand-authored quests, dialogue trees, or set pieces. Everything generated by systems or performed by players.
_Novelty_: A solo developer cannot author a city but can grow one.

**[Constraint #65]: One District First**
_Core Loop_: Launch a neighbourhood, not a metropolis. The city expands as the game earns it.
_Novelty_: Diegetically invisible, and makes the always-ticking server bill survivable pre-revenue.

### Emergence

**[Emergence #95]: Rush Hour From Schedules** — LIKED
_Core Loop_: Shift times alone produce congestion, delay, risk, incident, and institutional response.
_Novelty_: The founding traffic-light example emerges from one primitive, unauthored.

**[Emergence #96]: Player Gentrification** — LIKED
_Core Loop_: Physical state to desirability to rent to demographics to physical state. Players are the input.
_Novelty_: A genuine socioeconomic dynamic arising unauthored from four simple rules.

**[Emergence #97]: Reputation Without A System** — LIKED
_Core Loop_: Individual agents remember individual people. No global score exists.
_Novelty_: Unfarmable social standing, because there is nothing to farm.

**[Emergence #98]: The Geographic Social Graph** — LIKED
_Core Loop_: Time-cost of travel means friendships form along routines and proximity.
_Novelty_: Community structure emerges from the commute rather than a friends list.

**[Emergence #99]: Understudy Drift** — BOUNDED by #103
_Core Loop_: Extended absence produces a character shaped by the AI's choices.
_Novelty_: Identity as something that erodes when unattended. Must never produce catastrophe.

**[Emergence #100]: Labour Market Saturation** — LIKED
_Core Loop_: Players flooding a popular job depress availability and wages; unpopular roles pay better.
_Novelty_: Self-balancing career economy with no designer intervention.

**[Emergence #101]: Institutional Decay From Churn** — REJECTED, see #104
_Core Loop_: Abandoned player-held roles slow their chains.
_Novelty_: Rejected because world condition should never be an artifact of player count.

**[Correction #104]: Decay Is Political, Not Demographic** — ACCEPTED
_Core Loop_: Unfilled roles are always backfilled by AI. Services degrade only when someone chooses it — a worker who will not work, a councillor who defunds the route.
_Novelty_: Decay always has an author. The city never rots because the server was quiet.

**[Emergence #102]: Emergent Norms** — WAIT AND SEE
_Core Loop_: Players invent conventions — queueing, tipping, a usual table, a standing Sunday ride.
_Novelty_: Culture as content the developer never writes.

## Themes and Patterns

**Significance without centrality.** The founding fantasy — one part of a massive thing — survives from the first minute to hour 100. Where most games invert their premise at endgame (nobody becomes master), this one deepens it: you end up load-bearing, still a part.

**Institutions are the unifying engine.** The bureaucratic chain is simultaneously world simulation, job system, narrative generator, failure mechanism, and endgame. Nearly every idea in this session either feeds it or falls out of it.

**Consequence must have a physical carrier.** Litter spreads because litter is there. Impoliteness does not spread because nothing lies in the street. This single rule eliminates morality meters, reputation auras, and invisible simulation — and it produced Broken Windows, emergent reputation, and the crime model.

**Time is the real currency.** The compressed clock makes minutes scarce, which makes transport upgrades meaningful, dialogue costly, jail and hospital punishing, and the commute a genuine decision.

**Reciprocal occupancy.** AI covers players, players cover AI. One mechanic answers absence, sleep, population density, the AI/player blend, and institutional fault-tolerance.

**Constraints turned out to be the design.** The 1-second browser boot enforces the no-protagonist premise. The pixel tilesets make local density affordable. The solo agentic team forces systemic content, which is what a sandbox wanted anyway. The compressed clock makes a lifetime cheap to author.

**Freedom scales inversely with supervision.** The most striking finding of the session: the emptiest job has the most agency, because nobody is watching. How a player spends an unsupervised night is the only characterisation a game with no opinion can offer.

**Cheap is not fake.** Reduce resolution, never causality.

## Promising Combinations

**The plastic bottle is the vertical slice.** A full bin (sanitation budget shortfall) produces a dropped bottle (physical carrier) which licenses more (broken windows) which degrades a district (emergent character) which triggers complaints (municipal memory) which starts a budget chain (bureaucracy) staffed by people players can be. One object exercises institutions, emergence, jobs, physical consequence, and the AI/player swap — and it scopes to a single street.

**The unlocked door closes the loop.** A negligent night guard skips the back door round. That is not punished — it is simply a door nobody checked, which is exactly where opportunity-based crime grows. The next morning an incident report is filed, a chain starts, and a budget line for a second guard gets discussed in a building the guard has never entered.

**Sleep plus multiplayer plus realism resolved into one mechanic.** The night shift turned the hardest constraint collision in the design into content, income, night population, and the mechanical proof of seamless AI/player mixing.

**The bike closes its own circle.** The opening example — bike in a shop window, leading to a cycling community, leading to the wooded north — became mechanically motivated once time is currency: the bike refunds fifteen minutes of commute, and those minutes are what you spend discovering the community that shows you the north.

**Granularity generates co-op.** Two people and a sofa is only a co-op verb if carrying has procedure. The realism pillar produces the multiplayer content rather than competing with it.

**The endgame fulfils the opening.** Institutional and proprietary progression are the same mechanic — becoming a dependency others route through — which means the hour-100 player is still one part of a massive thing.

## Session Summary

### Most Promising Concepts

**Top Pick: The City As Institution — the bureaucratic chain as unifying engine (#10, #11, #12)**

The single most productive idea of the session. City responses to events are simulated as multi-agent institutional processes — investigator, sign-off, department, budget, procurement, supplier, warehouse, work crew — and every link is a job a citizen holds, AI or player. It resolves five separate design problems at once: it makes the city visibly "breathe," it supplies the job system, it generates emergent narrative through institutional friction, it provides the failure mechanism (ruin arrives as paperwork), and it becomes the endgame (you climb into the machine and become a link others wait on). It also happens to be the cheapest thing in the design to simulate, because a chain is a sequence of records rather than rendered bodies.

**Runner-up: Reciprocal Occupancy — the understudy and the night shift (#13, #23, #24)**

AI covers players when they log off; players cover AI during their character's sleep. One mechanic answers absence, the sleep problem, night-time population, the seamless AI/player blend, and institutional fault tolerance. It began as a solution to "what happens when you close the tab" and ended up load-bearing for the entire MMO architecture.

**Honourable Mention: Boring Jobs Are Free Jobs (#88, #89)**

The session's most counter-intuitive finding. Low-action work means low supervision, which means maximum self-determination — so the emptiest shift in the city offers the most player agency. In a game with no morality system, how a player spends an unsupervised night is the only characterisation available. This inverted the assumption that engagement requires density of demands, and produced a reusable shift template (ritual open, rhythmic duties, discretionary middle, ritual close) that generalises to every job in the city.

### Key Insights

- **The fantasy survives the endgame.** Most games invert their premise at hour 100 (the nobody becomes the master). This one deepens it — you become load-bearing while remaining one part of a massive thing.
- **Constraints turned out to be the design, not obstacles to it.** The 1-second browser boot enforces the no-protagonist premise (there is no time for a character creator). The pre-existing pixel tilesets make local density affordable. The solo agentic team forces systemic content, which a sandbox wanted anyway.
- **Two design laws emerged that will decide most future arguments:** consequences require a physical carrier (litter spreads, impoliteness does not), and resolution scales while causality does not (cheap is never fake).
- **Immersion is the terminal value; realism is instrumental.** This makes granularity negotiable — spend it where an action has procedure and can be done badly, abstract it everywhere else.
- **The player's own examples encoded the design before it was articulated.** The bike in the shop window became mechanically motivated once time became the currency; the traffic light chain turned out to emerge from work schedules alone.

### Design Challenges

1. **The Burger Test is unvalidated.** The entire design rests on mundane work being intrinsically satisfying. Until a job is prototyped and played, this is a hypothesis.
2. **The 1-second boot against MMO streaming.** Boot budget including load is the hardest technical constraint in the project and it has not been designed against.
3. **Always-ticking server economics.** A continuously simulated city costs money whether or not anyone is playing. Priced by the developer, but it constrains district one's size.
4. **Steering the optimiser (#109) needs its metrics designed.** The decision is to redirect min-maxers by making the visibly accruing quantities the right ones — districts seen, people who know you, procedures mastered. Those quantities do not yet exist.
5. **The borrowing licence (#42) is unproven as an anti-griefing measure.** Consequence for borrowed bodies lands on the NPC, not the player; the licence is the proposed fix but has not been stress-tested.
6. **Gentrification (#96) is liked but undampened.** It will price newest players out of the best districts unless rent caps or social housing are introduced — both of which are institutions, and therefore content.

### Open Questions

- **What are the goals besides wealth?** The wealth ladder does not terminate, and is explicitly one goal among several — but the others have not been enumerated. This is the most important open item for the brief.
- **Can a player take the same night shift repeatedly** and become known as the night bus driver, accruing a second reputation? Or do shifts stay anonymous?
- **Is the night shift framed diegetically as a dream, or as a shift board?** Bears on UX-Minimal Diegesis (#9).
- **Which jobs are player-accessible?** Job access tiers (#94) is a tuning dial, and the first ten playable roles are undecided.
- **How big is district one, and how many citizens does it hold?** Density is local (#69), so this is a server-budget question rather than a feel question — but it is still unanswered.
- **Where exactly does granularity apply?** The admission test exists (#35: can the action be done badly?) but has not been applied to a job list.
- **Do emergent norms (#102) actually appear?** Wait-and-see; only observable with real players.

### Settled Decisions

- **No death.** Injury, hospital, debt, and jail are real; characters persist. (#82)
- **Players stay lawful in v1.** Crime exists in the world; players engage the response side. Revisited with data. (#86, #87)
- **The understudy is conservative.** It maintains your life, never gambles with it. (#103)
- **Decay is political, not demographic.** AI backfills every unfilled role; services degrade only when someone chooses it. (#104)
- **Steer the optimiser rather than cap them.** (#109)
- **Browser is a hard commitment**, with a sub-one-second boot including load.
- **Simulation fidelity is the highest-priority pillar**, ahead of granularity.

### Recommended Next Steps

1. **Create the Game Brief** (`gds-create-game-brief`) using this session as input. The material is well past the density a brief needs, and the open questions above are exactly what a brief exists to resolve.
2. **Prototype the plastic bottle loop on a single street.** It exercises institutions, emergence, jobs, physical consequence, and the AI/player swap in the smallest possible scope, and it directly tests the Burger Test and the Truth Test.
3. **Enumerate the non-wealth goals** before the GDD. This is the one open question that changes the shape of the design rather than its details.
